Hope & Wellbeing

Every brain has strengths

Calm, practical ideas for feeling a little better day to day, for neurodivergent people and for the people who love and support them.

StrengthsCalming tipsSmall stepsFor caregivers

Different, with strengths

Being neurodivergent means your brain works differently. Difference can bring real strengths.

  • Detail and focus

    Many autistic adults describe strengths such as noticing details others miss and focusing deeply on what they love.[research]

  • Energy and ideas

    Adults with ADHD have described curiosity, creative thinking, energy and courage as positive parts of how they think.[research]

  • Connection that fits

    Communication can flow easily between people who think alike. Finding “your people” matters.[research]

Calming tips to try

Try one at a time and keep what works for you. Everyone is different.

  • Slow breathing

    Breathe in gently through your nose for 4, then out slowly for 6. Repeat a few times. A longer out-breath can help your body settle.

  • 5-4-3-2-1

    Notice 5 things you see, 4 you can touch, 3 you hear, 2 you smell, and 1 you taste. It brings attention back to the here and now.

  • A sensory kit

    Keep a small bag with what helps you: headphones, sunglasses, something soft, a fidget, a snack, a water bottle.

  • A quiet corner

    A calm, dim spot to go to before feelings get too big. Rest is not giving up.

  • Know what’s next

    Picture schedules, timers, and a heads-up before changes make the day more predictable.

  • Move a little

    A short walk, stretching, swinging, or jumping can help the body reset.

  • Name the feeling

    Putting a word to a feeling (“I feel worried”) can make it a little smaller. Children can practice with our Feelings game.

  • Recharge time

    After a busy or social day, plan quiet time to recharge. Needing it is normal.

  • Special interests

    Time with a favorite topic or hobby can bring joy and calm. It is not “too much”.

Small steps count

  • Progress is rarely a straight line. A hard day does not erase the good ones.
  • Celebrate small wins: a new word, a calm morning, one more star.
  • You do not have to do everything. Pick one small thing for today.
  • Asking for help is a strength. No one should do this alone.

For parents & caregivers: you matter too

Caregiving can be a lot, and research shows the stress is real.[research] Looking after yourself is part of looking after them.

  • Tiny breaks

    Five quiet minutes, a cup of tea, or a short walk can help. Small and often works better than waiting for a big break.

  • Share the load

    Let others help with specific things. Local Parent Centers can help with school questions.

  • Find your people

    Other parents understand. We are planning calm parent groups. Join the waitlist.

If things feel too hard

You deserve support. If you or someone else is in danger, call your local emergency number. In the United States, call or text 988 (988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline). In other countries, see Find A Helpline.

These tips are general ideas, not medical advice. A doctor or therapist can help you find what fits you.
